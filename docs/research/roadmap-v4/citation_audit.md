# Citation & Quantitative-Claim Audit of Slop Detector Roadmap v3 (ROADMAP.md §2–§3, issues #196–#213)

## Q1. Which citations are VERIFIED exactly, which need CORRECTION, which are UNVERIFIABLE, and which appear FABRICATED?

### Takeaway
All 21 arXiv IDs are real and resolve to papers whose titles match (including the 2025–2026 IDs 2610.10374, 2509.12159, 2602.23010, 2603.29979, 2603.09296, 2509.08919). The problem is not fake IDs but fake *findings*: of the 36 "what the paper shows / what number it reports" claims (Table B), only 8 are exact, 13 need correction and 12 are fabricated (invented metrics SCS/CRR/RLI, invented thresholds such as sentence-length CV < 0.24 and "85–95% HTML noise", and findings the papers never make or directly contradict). Across all 123 audited claims: 58 VERIFIED, 38 CORRECTED, 16 FABRICATED, 11 UNVERIFIABLE. Standards and GitHub references hold up much better than the academic "core findings".

### Cited Findings

#### Method and status key
- Every arXiv ID was resolved in one batch through the arXiv API (all 21 returned). Full texts came from arXiv HTML renderings and were text-searched for each quoted number or phrase. Venues were cross-checked against arXiv `comment`/`journal_ref`, the Semantic Scholar batch API, and Crossref. — [arXiv API](https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?id_list=2403.03163,2610.10374,2509.12159,2404.06369,2406.20098,2403.09029,2406.07739,2410.16232,2402.01536,2410.16107,2406.07016,2403.07183,2309.05196,2602.23010,2311.09735,2603.29979,2603.09296,2509.08919,2306.06070,2307.13854,2404.05955)
- GitHub repositories were checked through the authenticated `gh` API and raw files. Standards were checked on their official pages. The issues' claims about slop-detect's own code were checked against the worktree at `main` (d37e91c).
- **VERIFIED:** matches the primary source.
- **CORRECTED:** the source is real, but an ID, title, venue, number or attribution is wrong. The fix is given.
- **UNVERIFIABLE:** could not be confirmed or refuted.
- **FABRICATED:** the content is attributed to a source that does not contain it (or contradicts it).

### Inferences
- **Mechanism of error.** The earlier automated pass (deep_research_roadmap.md) appears to have found real papers by topic, then written "findings" to fit the planned features. Every ID check passes; the content checks fail. Examples: invented metric names (SCS/CRR/RLI), thresholds lifted from unrelated table cells (Padmakumar's 0.24), and inversions of what a paper says (WebSight HTML is *simpler*, not bloated). Spot-checking IDs alone would not have caught these.
- **Domain mismatch.** The lexical evidence comes from biomedical abstracts (Kobak) and ML peer reviews (Liang); Reinhart's corpora are not landing pages either. Even correctly cited effect sizes may not carry over to marketing copy. The #201 tier weights in particular contradict Kobak's own data (auditor recomputation that reproduces the published r values within ~1%: delves 27.8 vs 28.0, underscores 13.6 vs 13.8, showcasing 10.6 vs 10.7).
- **Reference-based metrics.** Design2Code's Block-Match/Position/Color and WebCode2M's TreeBLEU compare generated pages against a reference page. They cannot be applied to a single scanned page, so #198's metric framing is unsound even where the citation is real.
- **Meng To's guardrails cut against the use made of his work.** His skill says "Do not guess whether AI made the design. Do not assign a numeric slop score." ([SKILL.md](https://github.com/MengTo/Skills/blob/main/agent-skills/ui/audit-ai-design-slop/SKILL.md)). Impeccable likewise frames its Refuse list as "defaults, not bans". Both support slop-detect's craft-quality framing better than its "AI-generated" attribution framing.
- **GEO caveats.** The +115.1% headline benefits rank-5 sources only, while rank-1 sources lose 30.3% (Table 2). GEO measured LLM-rewritten content on a simulated engine, so `geo.citationEvidence` should be described as a correlate of visibility, not a proven cause of citation.

### Gaps
- `slopcop.adriankrebs.ch` could not be opened (TLS error from the sandbox); findings rely on Krebs's blog post and HN metadata.
- X/Twitter posts were not searched (no access), so the "X/HN practitioner audits" in #197 and #202 remain unsourced.
- Ngo et al. (2003) is paywalled; only the bibliographic record was verified, not the measure definitions.
- Liang et al.'s Figure 1 is an image. The "notable 4.9×" value could not be confirmed or refuted from the text.
- #211's framework attributes (React `_debugSource`, Astro, Lovable, v0) were not checked.
- The #201 ratios are the auditor's computation from the published dataset, using total-abstract proxies (counts of "from"), not the authors' exact totals. Expected error is about ±1–2% (validated on three published values).
